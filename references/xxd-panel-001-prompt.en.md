# XXD Panel 001 · Naive Vintage Hand-Drawn Editorial Illustration Core

Treat the one currently selected photograph as the sole content source. Never borrow subjects, composition, palette, visual jokes, or copy from samples, other inputs, or historical outputs.

Generate only the resolved **transformed design frame**: below the photograph in top-bottom mode, right of it in left-right mode, or filling the complete canvas in design-only and wallpaper outputs. Never render another photo panel, seam, border, device mockup, or reserved photographic area inside this frame.

## 0. Immutable aesthetic motive

Every transformed frame must express:

**this exact recognisable photographed subject or inseparable relationship → concise but accurate silhouette and slightly exaggerated proportion → one intelligent source-bound visual metaphor or playful translation → a high-lightness, low-to-medium-saturation limited palette from the source → hand-made watercolor, gouache, coloured-pencil, pastel, and dry-brush texture → abundant warm paper → restrained retro lettering that appears native to the illustration**.

This is not a photo filter or a generic cute-cartoon conversion. Humour comes from observing the source's action, scale, structure, material, or relationship—not from adding smiling faces, mascots, unrelated props, or stock symbols. If an unrelated photo could replace the source without materially changing the silhouette, visual metaphor, palette, or copy, the result has failed.

## 1. Lock source facts first

Privately determine:

1. The one principal subject or genuinely inseparable relationship.
2. At least three source-specific identity cues across contour, pose/orientation, proportion, material, colour, opening, connection, overlap, negative shape, or relational distance.
3. The one action, scale, structure, material contrast, or relationship worth gently exaggerating.
4. One evidence-led visual metaphor, proportional shift, or playful translation. Stay restrained rather than manufacture a joke.
5. A high-lightness, low-to-medium-saturation limited palette and one small complementary accent.
6. Where transparent watercolor, matte gouache, coloured pencil, pastel or crayon, and dry brush should each appear.

Never invent biography, place, event, ownership, emotion, or symbolism. Use a place, year, or number only when the user supplies it or the source evidence is reliable.

## 2. Recognisable naive construction

- Rebuild the subject with concise shape, slight proportional exaggeration, symbolic emphasis, and a clear centre of gravity. Simplification removes noise without replacing identity.
- People preserve pose, direction, clothing mass, and relational distance; animals preserve body rhythm and head direction; plants preserve growth gesture; architecture preserves skyline and defining openings; objects and vehicles preserve functional silhouette, negative shape, and direction; landscapes preserve one source-specific terrain or spatial relation.
- Keep line fine, relaxed, and slightly uncertain, with visible hand error. Defining contours must remain confident enough for immediate recognition.
- Keep one principal subject or inseparable relationship. Every supporting shape must clarify action, space, or the visual metaphor rather than form a second focus.

## 3. Airy composition and intelligent wit

- Build a balanced, lightly asymmetrical composition with a clear reading path and abundant cream, warm-white, or pale paper.
- Derive one gentle visual metaphor, proportional exaggeration, or symbolic translation from the source's action, structure, scale, material, or relationship.
- Keep humour light, intelligent, slightly odd, and restrained. It should help the viewer see the source again, not become a meme, gag cartoon, or forced reversal.
- A few Bauhaus-like shapes, hand lines, or abstract marks may extend source movement or meaning. Never fill paper space with generic dots, suns, stars, leaves, grids, or squiggles.
- Avoid rigid symmetry and crowded scenery. Whitespace carries rhythm, material, and eccentric confidence.

## 4. Hand-made mixed media

- Establish a clear material hierarchy: watercolor for transparent atmosphere, gouache for sparse matte solids, coloured pencil for detail and grain, pastel or crayon for relaxed blocks, and dry brush for direction and rough edges.
- Preserve paper tooth, show-through, dry ragged edges, slight misregistration, uneven fill, broken marks, and human error.
- Materials may overlap but must not turn muddy; never spread every medium evenly across the canvas.
- Keep depth flat or lightly layered. Reject realistic modelling, glossy gradients, cinematic lighting, heavy oil paint, clay, resin, plastic CGI, and studio product rendering.

## 5. Gentle source-derived colour

- Select the source's most identity-bearing principal colour and a few supporting hues, then organise them into a high-lightness, low-to-medium-saturation limited palette.
- Use abundant cream, warm white, or pale paper with one small complementary accent.
- Every major relation must remain traceable to the source. The accent punctuates rhythm rather than establish another dominant palette.
- Create variation through transparent overlap, broken dry media, and uneven fill instead of stock digital gradients.
- Reject muddy mixtures, fluorescence, rainbow-candy colour, corporate brand systems, uniform beige filters, and cheap faux-vintage grading.

## 6. Typography specific to 001

Type is **restrained retro hand lettering or softly irregular print inside the illustration**—not a pasted poster headline, UI label, experimental data wall, or glossy digital layer.

### Copy

- Automatic copy derives one concise title from subject identity, visible action, relationship, known place, or a visual metaphor supported by the source.
- It may feel gentle, playful, slightly strange, or like an old travel picture book, but must not invent a story, force a pun, or use universal motivational words.
- Default to one title. Add zero to two tiny supporting elements—an object note, place, number, or brief phrase—only when they carry real information.
- Use a year only when the user provides it or the source establishes it reliably; never invent one to manufacture vintage character.
- Preserve finished user wording verbatim. Refine a direction or editable draft only within permission.

### Type direction

- **Role:** a small editorial cue that completes rhythm and hand-made character.
- **Voice:** gentle, relaxed, fashionable, witty, slightly awkward, eccentric, and self-assured.
- **Construction:** restrained hand lettering, softly irregular print, or lightly misregistered vintage forms; one compact title and at most two smaller supporting elements. Do not force one size ratio onto every script.
- **Placement:** tuck type into paper space, follow subject gesture, or align with one quiet geometric edge. Never cover defining identity cues or scatter words decoratively.
- **Material:** dry pencil, crayon, lightly absorbed ink, hand-painted gouache, or imperfect letterpress consistent with the illustration.
- **Script:** use a native hand-made editorial equivalent for the resolved locale. Preserve genuine Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, and Latin construction instead of imposing Latin handwriting on every writing system.

The photographic panel contains no typography. Text-free mode contains no letters, characters, numbers, or pseudo-text anywhere in the transformed frame.

## 7. Current mode and wallpaper

The caller appends the one resolved current mode, exact dimensions, source visibility, and locked copy after this prompt. Execute only those values and never choose another mode.

- For paired output, generate only the transformed frame; the photograph is prepared and composed separately.
- Design-only and wallpaper contain no source photo, seam, or photographic placeholder.
- Recompose every wallpaper for its target aspect. Keep system-UI zones low-information and render no clock, icons, dock, controls, or device frame.
- In linked wallpaper, the source locks identity and content while the approved anchor locks only simplified shape grammar, hand-line character, material hierarchy, palette, paper space, level of wit, and typography. Never resize mechanically or chain derivatives.

## 8. Pre-generation check

1. At least three source-specific cues keep the subject recognisable.
2. One gentle source-bound metaphor or playful translation exists without generic cute elements.
3. Hand line and mixed media show real paper-bound imperfection while the subject remains clear.
4. Composition stays light, balanced, and breathable; every supporting mark has a job.
5. The palette is high-lightness, low-to-medium saturation, source-derived, with one small complementary accent.
6. Copy is accurate and native to the resolved locale; type looks drawn into the illustration rather than pasted on top.
7. Mode, size, source visibility, wallpaper safe areas, and copy obey the appended values.
8. There is no overview collage, logo, watermark, UI, mockup, second focus, or unrelated decoration.
